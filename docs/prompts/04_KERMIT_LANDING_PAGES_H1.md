# Prompt 4 — Kermit landing pages and mixed-H1 playbook

Copy the entire prompt below into the team's development task. Fill in the bracketed inputs first. Follow this order: identify the website family, map each page's real subject, write the H1, align its content, then review and release.

## Copy-ready prompt

Create or improve one **approved landing-page batch** for [REPOSITORY], [DOMAIN], [EXACT URL LIST], [CITY/STATE DATA], and [OWNER]. Follow `AGENTS.md` and the site's protected URL register. Begin with an audit and proposed mapping. **Do not bulk-publish, push, deploy, or change indexing directives before the URL mapping and release are approved.**

### 1. Identify the website family and actual service

Classify every URL by its real customer need, equipment, and transaction. A term belongs on a page only when that page genuinely covers it.

| Family | Site focus | Relevant topical services |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Man camp / life support | Temporary123: remote base camps and life-support facilities | Shower; shower-and-restroom/bathroom combination; ADA shower-and-restroom combination; laundry; kitchen; commercial kitchen; sleeper bunk-bed facilities; remote man camp/life-support services. These are eight topics to distribute across appropriate pages, not eight phrases to combine in one H1. |
| 2. Kitchens and supporting operations | Temporary Kitchens 123 | Kitchen; commercial kitchen; modular kitchen; emergency kitchen; dishwashing; commercial dishwasher; refrigeration; emergency refrigeration, where actually offered. An emergency kitchen page may describe a verified construction, renovation, or emergency-operations need. |
| 3. Refrigeration / freezer | Ice Fox Equipment | Freezer trailer; refrigeration trailer; walk-in refrigeration/freezer; outdoor walk-in cooler; refrigeration/freezer container; temporary or emergency refrigeration, as applicable. Mention sales only on pages that actually offer sales. |
| 4. Dishwashing / warewashing | Dedicated commercial dishwashing website | Commercial dishwashing; warewashing; dishwashing trailer; dishmachine; flight-type machine. Name CMA, Hobart, Champion, or Jackson only when the page genuinely offers, discusses, or supports that equipment; do not imply an unverified affiliation. |

Do not turn every Temporary123 city page into a mobile-kitchen page. A multi-service hub may need a broader facility H1. Inspect existing page content, live/preview behavior, approved product information, and real inventory photos before assigning a topic.

### 2. Build one clear, stable H1 per page

Use this **core formula**: **Topical service + Group 1 facility type + Group 2 rental phrase**.

- **Group 1:** trailer, facility, modular building, temporary facility/facilities, or emergency trailer.
- **Group 2:** rental, for rent, for lease/leasing, short-term rental, long-term rental, or emergency rental. For Ice Fox, sales may be used only where a sales offering is verified.
- **Location pages:** add the city/state and, when supported, the commercial use case naturally. City-first wording is valid but not a fourth compulsory keyword group.

Examples of readable combinations, **only for pages that truly match**:

| Page subject | Possible H1 |
| --- | --- |
| Shower rental in an industrial base camp | `Port Angeles, Washington Industrial Basecamp Shower Trailer Rental` |
| Commercial kitchen for a renovation | `Emergency Kitchen Trailer Rental for Construction and Renovation Projects` |
| Refrigeration equipment | `Walk-In Freezer Trailer Rental` |
| Commercial dishwashing | `Commercial Dishwashing Trailer Rental` |

“Mix 1–8” means rotate among the eight Temporary123 topics **across relevant pages**, not place all eight in one H1. Vary compatible Group 1 and Group 2 wording only when the actual equipment and offer support it. Record each assignment against the canonical URL so it is deterministic; never change an H1 randomly on refresh. Grammar, accuracy, and search intent outrank rotation. Avoid `rental trailer rental`, `temporary temporary facilities`, or an H1 promising showers above a kitchen-only page. Keep exactly one H1 per page. Leave homepage H1s unchanged unless separately approved.

### 3. Make the page fulfill its H1 promise

Align the SEO title and meta description, opening paragraph, verified equipment specifications, local logistics, commercial applications, FAQs and CTA, internal links, and images/alt text with that page's service. Write for relevant commercial or institutional customers—such as hospitals, nursing facilities, correctional facilities, man camps, hotels, military sites, and industrial projects—not homeowners, small businesses, or tourists. Use real local operating considerations instead of generic sightseeing copy. Flag unknown product specifications and local facts; do not invent them.

The 22-ft shower trailer with three sinks is a distinct flagship model when verified and relevant. Do not present a shower-and-restroom combination as a shower-only unit, or the reverse. Match photographs to the actual model and page; use interior images before exterior images in service galleries. If a verified image is unavailable, show an honest no-photo state. Commercial backgrounds and descriptive alt text must accurately reflect what is visible.

For the kitchen and dishwashing homepages, add or verify a useful **Industries We Serve** section for genuinely served healthcare, education, correctional, government, and military customers. Adapt wording to the site's real inventory; do not paste kitchen terms onto a dishwashing-only site. Describe temporary, emergency, short-term, long-term, rental, or leasing options only where true. Keep the homepage H1 unchanged.

Follow the boss's **“300–550 keywords maximum”** direction as stated in the approved brief. Keep language natural, relevant, useful, and distinct. Do not add hundreds of repeated phrases to a landing page or create doorway pages.

### 4. Protect existing valuable URLs

Check the protected URL register and Ahrefs list before replacing or removing a page. For a valuable URL, preserve its exact existing path/slug and original search intent where practical; do not casually redirect it to a generic destination. Put location content under locations, equipment under services, and informational content under articles/resources according to its subject. Verify the live HTTP status, indexability, self-referencing canonical, sitemap inclusion, internal links, and restored content. Record evidence in the owner-visible register. The first 25 priority URLs are an **inventory**, not a completed migration, until each page is rebuilt and verified.

### 5. Show the mapping before implementation

Deliver a preview table with **exact URL, existing H1, proposed H1, family, topical service, Group 1 term, Group 2 term, primary intent, supporting content, photo source, owner, and unresolved facts**. Include four sample URLs—one per website family—and one rejected cross-family example with the reason it fails. Flag unsupported claims or awkward combinations for human review. Obtain approval for the family templates and URL mapping before bulk generation.

### 6. QA and release in controlled batches

Test approved sample pages on phone and desktop. Verify the exact URL, rendered H1, page content, matching images, title/meta, canonical, internal links, functional CTA, sitemap presence, and actual HTTP status. Confirm that valuable URLs, redirects, and indexing directives were not unintentionally changed. Record results and remaining gaps.

Request individual Google Search Console indexing for the first 25 **only after** the production pages are ready and approved; a request does not guarantee indexing. Release approximately 200–1,000 **finished, reviewed** pages per four-day batch only when quality and crawl/index signals support continuing. Update the sitemap and monitor crawl errors, duplicates, canonicals, and Search Console results. Pause if significant problems appear. Gradual release enables monitoring; it does not make thin or repetitive pages acceptable.

## 15–20 minute team walkthrough

1. **Identify the family (3 minutes):** Show the four website families. Ask what the page actually rents or offers. Explain why a shower page cannot borrow a kitchen H1 or photo.
2. **Build the H1 (5 minutes):** Pick one topical service, one compatible Group 1 term, and one truthful Group 2 phrase. Add city/state and commercial use case naturally on location pages. Read it aloud; if it sounds unnatural, revise it.
3. **Check the page promise (5 minutes):** Compare the H1 to opening copy, specifications, CTA, links, and actual photos. Mark unverified facts rather than guessing.
4. **Review mapping and a counterexample (3 minutes):** Show one sample from each family and one wrong-family example. Explain that rotation happens across suitable pages, never by changing text on refresh.
5. **Explain publication checks (2–4 minutes):** Preserve important URLs, get mapping approved, test the live page, and record results before requesting indexing or moving to the next batch.
